Cells

Two references to one value. Edit either one.

  1. 1Not yet completed: Change either 7. Watch the other one change too.
  2. 2Not yet completed: Click a comma and type ( to create a new cell. Click the empty box inside it, type 11, then press Enter.
  3. 3Not yet completed: Click another comma to open a space. Hold Ctrl and click a parenthesis around your new cell—not the number—to put another reference there.
  4. 4Not yet completed: Change either 11. Its new partner changes too; the original pair stays separate.

Parentheses select the reference; the inside edits its contents.

Grap

  1. 1Not yet completed: Change the 2 after + to 4. The sum becomes 7; the multiplication stays at 6.
  2. 2Not yet completed: Change any parenthesized 3 to 5. All three references update. After the first step, the results become 9 and 10.

Functions

scale multiplies its input by 2. Each call supplies its own x.

  1. 1Not yet completed: Change the first call’s 3 to 4. Its result becomes 8; the other stays at 10.
  2. 2Not yet completed: In the function definition, change the multiplier 2 to 3. After the first step, both results update to 12 and 15.
  3. 3Not yet completed: Click x inside the definition’s parameter list and rename it to amount. Its uses and the call labels change too; the results stay the same.

References use identity, not spelling. Renaming keeps them connected.

Drawing

  1. 1Not yet completed: In the first dot call under do, change 60 to 80. Only the left circle moves.
  2. 2Not yet completed: In the function definition, change radius from 24 to 36. Both circles grow.
  3. 3Not yet completed: Hold Ctrl and click either circle to select the fill call that drew it.
