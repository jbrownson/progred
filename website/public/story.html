This forest is a program. Drag the slider and it grows.

Progred is an editor where a program is structure, not text. What you see are drawings of that structure, and you can edit through any of them. This forest started as a single dot. Below, you’ll rebuild it step by step, strip it down to the records it’s made of, and end with a forest of your own.

A dot

This is the entire program: fill a circle with a color. The picture and the code are two drawings of the same thing, and each knows about the other.

  1. 1Not yet completed: Hold Ctrl and click the dot. Its code is selected.
  2. 2Not yet completed: Hold Ctrl and drag the 60 left and right. The dot slides as you drag, because 60 is a number you’re holding, not two characters.

Two dots

One dot isn’t a forest. Here are two, written out in full. The square brackets hold a list, and all draws everything in it.

  1. 1Not yet completed: Make both dots bigger.

That took two edits for one idea, and a forest would take fifty.

One recipe

So the dot becomes a recipe, a function. dot takes an x and fills in the rest, and each dot{x: …} below it is a call that supplies one. The → reads as “gives”. The circle inside changed too. The " makes it a template: it’s kept as written, except where ` marks a hole. Where the 60 was, `x is a hole each call fills with its own x.

  1. 1Not yet completed: Make both dots bigger again. It’s one edit now.
  2. 2Not yet completed: Hold Ctrl and click a dot. This time you land inside dot, on the fill that painted it. A click finds the deepest code on screen that drew what you clicked.

Dots become trees

The recipe got a trunk and green leaves, and every dot became a tree at once. But now the name is wrong.

  1. 1Not yet completed: In the recipe, click dot and rename it to tree.

Every call now says tree. Nothing was searched and replaced. So what is a call, if it doesn’t contain the name?

What a tree really is

Here are the same two calls, drawn three ways. Everything on screen is drawn by the editor’s libraries, plug-ins that each know how to draw one kind of thing: calls, numbers, names. Each view below has fewer of them, and each drawing is a projection of the same program. You can edit through any of them.

  1. 1Not yet completed: Click the 60 in the top view. It lights up in all three.
  2. 2Not yet completed: Now find it in the bottom view and click it there.

At the bottom nothing is interpreted. A call is a record, values filed under keys, and its keys are identities: …10115 is x, and the word x is a label drawn on top. 0x…4e40 is your 60, stored as the eight bytes of a floating-point number, and (…) is the recipe, folded; click the dots to open it. The recipe sits in the call as a cell, by identity, which is why renaming was safe. Everything in Progred is built from four things: records { }, lists [ ], blobs of raw bytes 0x…, and cells ( ), values with an identity of their own.

Taller and shorter

A forest where every tree is the same height looks like a fence. So the recipe got a height. The trunks follow it, but the leaves still sit at 160 - 60, where a 60-tall tree’s leaves go. (y counts down from the top, and the ground is at 160.)

  1. 1Not yet completed: Hold Ctrl and drag the 60 in the leaves’ 160 - 60 until the middle tree’s leaves sit on its trunk.

Now the other two are wrong. No single number fits trees of different heights: each tree’s leaves need what its trunk uses, its own height. In the next step, they have it.

Growing together

The leaves now sit at 160 - height, like the trunks, so each tree computes its own, the way a spreadsheet formula does. To make the forest grow, every tree’s height has to follow one shared number. The parentheses around each 1 mark a cell, a value with its own identity. All three calls hold the same one, the way three formulas can use one spreadsheet cell.

  1. 1Not yet completed: Change any of the 1s to 0.5, or hold Ctrl and drag it. Every tree shrinks at once.
  2. 2Not yet completed: Plant a fourth tree by hand: click between the last two tree lines, type tree, press Enter, and give it an x and a height.

Parameters have identities too: each call files its arguments under those same (x) and (height) cells, which is how the rename worked.

A forest

Now imagine planting fifty. So the forest counts them out instead. forest takes a tree count and a growth, and for each runs a small recipe once per tree. λ marks a recipe without a name, written right where it’s used; item is which tree it is, and sin gives each one its own height.

  1. 1Not yet completed: Set the tree count to 12.
  2. 2Not yet completed: Hold Ctrl and click any tree. You land in code you can read now.
